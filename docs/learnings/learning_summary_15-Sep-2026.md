# Learning Summary — 15 Sep 2026

## What We Achieved
- The check that compares what the AI read against the statement's own printed totals was fixed twice. A real statement from a fifth bank had been read correctly row by row, yet the check reported a disagreement: the AI had been asked for the "purchases total" and faithfully copied a subtotal with that label instead of the grand total. Renaming the question to "total debits" then produced nothing at all, because no line on the page is labelled that way.
- The question to the AI was changed from one that needs judgement — "which figure is the total?" — to one it can answer by copying: list every line in the statement's summary box with its amount and whether it is money in or money out. The app now adds those lines up itself and never takes a total from the AI as a single interpreted figure.
- The totals check gained a third result. It used to say "mismatch" both when the numbers disagreed and when there were no printed numbers to compare against; the second case now shows an amber "Unverified" badge with its own wording. Importing still needs an explicit "import anyway" in either case.
- The "Show Preview" window now shows every transaction whenever the totals check did not pass, instead of only the first and last few, because in that situation the rows are the only evidence the user has before deciding to import anyway. When it does show a sample, the divider states how many rows are shown out of how many.
- The screen for a statement from an unrecognised bank was redesigned from a developer-facing error — "unrecognised format", a list of bank names, and four controls that each did something different — into one flow: which card is this for, an optional AI-assisted switch, one Continue button. Choosing an existing card greys out the fields for a new one.
- The AI option became a sliding switch, and its explanation now says what leaves the machine: card and account numbers are removed first; transaction descriptions, dates and amounts are sent. When the AI service fails, the screen now says in plain words whether it was unavailable, unreachable, or simply could not make sense of the file, with the technical detail kept in small print.
- The automated suite grew from 763 to 788 passing checks over the day, with no failures at any step.

## How We Achieved It
- Every brief's stated assumptions were checked against the code before building, and the differences were written down at the time: a field the brief said to rename by subscript was in fact read by name; a claim that no test would change was true of the match and mismatch tests but not of four tests asserting the old "missing counts as mismatch" rule; a "clean tree" precondition that was not met stopped a session until the previous one was committed.
- Real statements were read to learn where the problem actually was. The fix to the totals question came from seeing that the AI had copied a labelled subtotal; the later proposal to remove names and addresses came from looking at how four banks actually print them — in six or more shapes, with no common marker — rather than assuming a single pattern.
- Wording shown to the user was held to what the code actually does. A supplied sentence claiming "no personal information is sent" was not shipped, because the cardholder's name is not removed today; the sentence that shipped says exactly what is removed and what is sent, and the stronger claim was recorded as work to do first.
- One sentence was added beyond a brief where a literal reading would have caused harm — the AI was told not to include balances and amounts due among the summary components, since every bank prints those next to the lines that should be summed — and the addition was flagged in the entry so it could be struck.
- Larger ideas that surfaced mid-session were parked in the project's open-flags list with a full design rather than folded into the session in hand, keeping each commit to one change.
- Where the browser tool was unavailable, the screen's script was parse-checked and its logic table-tested against sample inputs, and each entry states plainly that on-screen verification is manual.

## Key Learnings
1. **Ask the AI questions it can answer by copying, not by judging.** Twice the question "what is the total?" was answered with a slightly different figure than intended — first a labelled subtotal, then nothing. Asking for every line in the summary box and doing the arithmetic in the app removed the judgement call entirely.
2. **"Could not check" and "checked and wrong" are different findings.** Reporting both as a mismatch hid which one the user was looking at. Giving the first its own name and colour cost little and told the truth about what had happened.
3. **Show more evidence when there is less assurance.** A sample of rows is enough when the totals agree, because the printed figures vouch for the rest; when they do not, every row is the evidence, so every row is shown.
4. **A privacy sentence must describe the code, not the intention.** A well-meant "no personal information is sent" would have been false on the one screen where the user opts in to sending data out. Looking at what the code removes — and what the real statements contain — produced an accurate sentence and a concrete plan for the stronger one.
5. **A name that matches nothing on the page gets you nothing.** Renaming a field to be more correct made the AI return blanks, because it was still being asked to find a printed label. Correct naming and correct mechanism are different fixes; the second was the one needed.
